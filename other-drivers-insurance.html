Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

Short answer

No. The other driver's insurance company works for them, not you. Don't give a recorded statement, don't sign anything, and don't guess about fault. Tell them your attorney will contact them, and get a free case review first.

Who they work for

The adjuster from the other driver's insurance company is trying to keep what their company pays low. In Virginia, anything that suggests you were even partly at fault can hurt your claim. They may also offer a quick settlement. Don't accept it or sign a release before talking to an attorney: once you sign, you usually can't ask for more (see what if it was partly my fault).

Your own insurance is different

Your own policy may require you to report the crash to your own insurer, so report it and stick to the basic facts. If you may need to use your uninsured or underinsured coverage, your own insurer pays that claim too, so talk to an attorney before giving them a recorded statement.

If the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

Every Virginia auto policy includes uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. If the at-fault driver has no insurance, or not enough to cover your damages, your own policy may help pay.

Where this comes from: Va. Code § 38.2-2206

For policies starting on or after January 1, 2025, Virginia's minimum liability coverage is $50,000 per person, $100,000 per crash for injuries and $25,000 for property damage.

Where this comes from: Va. Code § 46.2-472
